# Inclusion and Accessibility Policy: human-managed accessibility programme activities and prompts

**Prepared:** 8 November 2026 · **Source revision:** `ecb72286ccc1e941e3dbe9a472f52b8f0cb29989`  
**Source:** [Double-FW/Inclusion-and-Accessibility-Policy](https://github.com/Double-FW/Inclusion-and-Accessibility-Policy/tree/ecb72286ccc1e941e3dbe9a472f52b8f0cb29989)  
**Audience:** accessibility programme leaders, executive sponsors, champions managers, policy owners, operational leaders, learning teams and research, AI, data and assurance partners.

## Where the repository is useful

Use this repository to connect applicable obligations, approved executive commitments and brand values with policy clauses, operational ownership, delivery evidence, change programmes and review. The policy-creation guide and companion human/AI RACIs support both specialist-team and embedded-responsibility organisations.

The guides and RACI allocations are proposals for local adoption. Policy drafting does not approve new commitments, expenditure, targets or exceptions, and applicable duties do not depend on executive restatement. Keep policy, detailed standards/procedures, funded plans and public service statements distinct. README descriptions of machine metadata and runtime controls are not evidence those mechanisms exist; the current AGENTS file explicitly limits that interpretation. Source-level rights notices differ and are retained.

The activities below are an editorial application of the repository, not a claim that it already contains a complete organisational process. AI prepares analysis, options, draft policy, delivery records and evidence summaries. Managers frame decisions, allocate resources and own implementation. Researchers involve affected people in questions, interpretation and evaluation. HR, data, finance, technical and risk specialists verify matters in their remit. Authorised leaders retain policy, employment, investment, publication and risk decisions. Assign these responsibilities to actual people locally; the examples do not establish a new team or approval body.

## How to use the prompts

1. Attach [RAG_Reference.md](RAG_Reference.md). For selective retrieval, use the source routes below and [Source_Coverage.md](Source_Coverage.md).
2. Replace bracketed inputs with the actual project brief, artefacts and evidence. Supply the target task, audience, platform/setting, constraints and intended decision. Treat missing information as unknown.
3. Use either example prompt for the activity. Both refer to this folder’s reference. Supply only appropriate project information; use anonymised research findings where possible.
4. Require source ID, filename and original section citations; distinguish source guidance, local proposal, observed result and uncertainty. Ask for evidence requests where an artefact cannot be inspected.
5. Review the result with the named human roles and record decisions. A prompt output is a draft management aid, not implementation or evidence that a test ran. Do not defer disabled people’s involvement until final testing.

**Reusable response contract:** “Use the attached RAG_Reference.md as the knowledge source. Cite source IDs, files and sections. Distinguish source statements, project evidence, recommendations and unknowns. Preserve applicable source identifiers. Do not invent research, capability, approvals or test results. Give a concrete management artefact, trade-offs, evidence requests and decisions for the human team.”

## 1. Assemble policy scope and authority

**Business process:** Programme governance and policy initiation

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_Creation_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Creation guide sections 1–2.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Define entities, jurisdictions, people, services and activities covered.
- Classify obligations, commitments, practice and proposed improvements.
- Separate transferable examples from locally adopted authority.

**Expected output:** A controlled policy brief and authority/source register.

**Human review:** Policy and executive owners agree scope; competent advisers verify jurisdiction-specific obligations.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the attached RAG to scope an inclusion policy for [organisation and supplied records]. Define boundaries and classify each source as an obligation requiring confirmation, approved commitment, verified practice, approved future work or proposal. Use the BBC example only for transferable relationships, not imported authority. Return evidence gaps and a controlled drafting brief.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [policy brief] with the RAG. Identify assumed jurisdictions, copied organisational provisions and ambitions with no approval. Return a corrected scope and the authority decisions needed before drafting.

## 2. Translate approved intent into traceable clauses

**Business process:** Policy development

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_Creation_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001).

**Relevant source sections:** Creation guide section 3; section 9.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Link each clause to its authority and intended human outcome.
- Identify delivery mechanisms, owners and evidence.
- Keep unsupported or conflicting clauses in a separate decision log.

**Expected output:** A proposed clause-to-authority/delivery matrix.

**Human review:** Policy, operational and executive owners verify each clause’s authority and feasible delivery.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG and [approved commitments, confirmed obligations and practice], build a policy clause matrix. For each proposed clause show authority, scope, intended experience, process, owner, evidence and unresolved decision. Do not invent adoption, implementation or budgets; separate gaps from operative wording.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [clause matrix] against the RAG. Identify requirements supported only by repository examples, policy claims that practice already exists and conflicting sources silently reconciled. Propose corrected wording and an explicit decision log.

## 3. Draft the right policy documents

**Business process:** Policy authorship and communications

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_Creation_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S005 — Policy-Generation-Prompt.md](RAG_Reference.md#s005).

**Relevant source sections:** Creation guide sections 1 and 4; Policy-Generation-Prompt.md.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Write concise governance-level policy rather than duplicating technical instructions.
- Separate policy from standards, funded plans and public status statements.
- Preserve scope, limitations and approval state.

**Expected output:** A candidate policy plus supporting delivery/gap records.

**Human review:** The policy owner coordinates specialist review; the authorised organisation owner adopts policy.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG and [approved drafting brief] to prepare candidate policy wording. Apply the generation prompt’s relevant inputs while separating policy, detailed standards/procedures, funded change plan and public accessibility statement. Return traceability and open decisions, label the policy draft and avoid new unapproved ambitions or performance claims.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [draft policy] against the RAG. Find technical detail in the wrong document, statements of actual delivery unsupported by evidence and plans disguised as adopted policy. Propose concise revisions and the supporting records needed.

## 4. Connect policy with funded change and deviations

**Business process:** Programme planning and scope management

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_Creation_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S004 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_RACI_and_Steering_Group_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s004).

**Relevant source sections:** Creation guide section 5; RACI guide sections 10 and 12.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Translate delivery gaps into programme actions and dependencies.
- Prevent approved commitments disappearing during reprioritisation.
- Prepare deviation/exception decisions with impact and review conditions.

**Expected output:** A policy-to-change map and draft deviation decision brief.

**Human review:** Delivery owners confirm feasible work; only delegated authorities can change commitments or accept risk.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, connect [policy clauses and delivery gaps] to [change portfolio]. Specify actions, receiving owners, dependencies, resources to confirm, interim access and evidence. Surface commitments threatened by scope changes and prepare explicit decisions rather than silently reducing policy ambition.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [programme rebaseline or supplier change] with the RAG. Identify commitments dropped without authority, temporary deviations without review and missing affected-person evidence. Produce a decision brief with options, impact, compensating action and proposed review/expiry.

## 5. Establish a proportionate steering mandate

**Business process:** Programme oversight

**Retrieve:** [S004 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_RACI_and_Steering_Group_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s004).

**Relevant source sections:** RACI guide sections 1–3 and 11.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Define scope, membership and links to existing governance.
- Document delegated and reserved matters, quorum and decision records.
- Provide accessible participation and separate confidential case handling.

**Expected output:** Proposed steering-group terms of reference and agenda.

**Human review:** The sponsor confirms actual delegation; affected people participate meaningfully and receive appropriate support.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to draft terms of reference for [existing or proposed governance forum]. Define scope, sponsor/chair, policy ownership, membership, lived-experience influence, reserved matters, quorum, decision records and escalation. Adapt suggested cadence locally and keep confidential individual cases outside routine strategic reporting.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [steering-group mandate] against the RAG. Identify consultation reduced to signatures, a forum claiming undelegated power or a new committee duplicating an existing one. Propose proportionate arrangements and unresolved sponsor decisions.

## 6. Allocate human responsibilities in specialist and embedded models

**Business process:** Programme operating-model design

**Retrieve:** [S004 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_RACI_and_Steering_Group_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s004).

**Relevant source sections:** RACI guide sections 2–6.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Map concrete activities to actual roles and named accountable owners.
- Keep one human A per activity and meaningful responsibility.
- Plan access to specialist capability where no dedicated team exists.

**Expected output:** A proposed human RACI and capability-gap record.

**Human review:** Each receiving function confirms its remit and capacity; RACI allocation does not itself confer authority.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, adapt the human RACI to [organisation roles and specialist availability]. Use specific policy-development, delivery and assurance activities, exactly one human A per row and at least one responsible human role. Mark missing delegation/capacity and identify specialist support without inventing a dedicated team.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [human RACI] against the RAG. Find multiple As, champions carrying every duty, responsibilities without time and consultation without influence. Propose corrected allocations and decisions for the actual owners.

## 7. Bound AI preparation and execution

**Business process:** AI-assisted programme governance

**Retrieve:** [S004 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_RACI_and_Steering_Group_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s004); [S003 — AGENTS.md](RAG_Reference.md#s003).

**Relevant source sections:** RACI guide sections 7–10; AGENTS: authority and evidence.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Separate agent work allocation from human responsibility.
- Specify authorised inputs, outputs, dependencies and review gates.
- Prevent draft preparation being reported as approval or enforcement.

**Expected output:** An AI-work allocation proposal linked to the human RACI.

**Human review:** Human owners approve actual task scope and access; the document does not configure permissions or live workflows.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to propose AI assistance for [policy/reporting tasks]. Link each agent task to the human RACI, authorised inputs, output, evidence dependencies, review and recovery. Distinguish drafting, checking and execution from approval. Do not imply repository prose implements permissions, audit storage or enforcement.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [agent workflow] against the RAG. Identify AI assigned human accountability, approval inferred from a status field, unmet evidence dependencies and stale or untrusted source instructions. Return a bounded preparation workflow and explicit human decisions.

## 8. Govern reference adoption and specialist handoffs

**Business process:** Programme knowledge management and procurement

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_Creation_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S004 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_RACI_and_Steering_Group_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s004).

**Relevant source sections:** Creation guide sections 10–11; RACI guide dependency sections.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Select operational references according to purpose and authority.
- Record versions, adoption scope and review triggers.
- Preserve standards, specialist advice and internal decisions as distinct sources.

**Expected output:** An adopted-reference register proposal and dependency map.

**Human review:** Policy and specialist owners confirm actual adoption; external links are not automatic organisational requirements.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, propose an operational reference register for [policy scope]. For each supplied reference record purpose, exact version, authority/adoption status, applicable activities, owner and review trigger. Route deeper specialist questions without copying unrelated guidance into policy or claiming unavailable sources were read.

### Example prompt 2

> Review [reference register] against the RAG. Identify a linked document treated as adopted, missing versions and technical guidance overriding approved commitments. Produce corrections and dependencies that need human confirmation.

## 9. Report policy delivery and human outcomes

**Business process:** Executive assurance and reporting

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_Creation_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S004 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_RACI_and_Steering_Group_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s004).

**Relevant source sections:** Creation guide section 7; RACI guide sections 6, 9 and 11.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Connect commitment, activity, evidence, experience and decision.
- Separate operational signals from verified outcomes.
- Protect individual cases and clarify measure definitions.

**Expected output:** A proposed executive report and evidence/claim register.

**Human review:** Evidence and policy owners review claims; the authorised report owner approves publication or escalation.

### Example prompt 1

> Use the RAG to prepare executive reporting for [policy period and evidence]. Show approved commitments, delivery, human outcomes, coverage limits, unresolved barriers, decisions and corrective actions. Define measures and denominators where relevant; separate signals from findings and exclude unnecessary individual case details.

### Example prompt 2

> Challenge [policy report] against the RAG. Identify adopted policy presented as proof of compliance, aggregated satisfaction hiding blocked tasks and unverified monitoring flags treated as incidents. Propose accurate wording, evidence requests and management actions.

## 10. Review policy alignment and control revisions

**Business process:** Policy lifecycle and organisational change

**Retrieve:** [S001 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_Creation_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s001); [S004 — Accessibility_and_Inclusion_Policy_RACI_and_Steering_Group_Guide.md](RAG_Reference.md#s004); [S003 — AGENTS.md](RAG_Reference.md#s003).

**Relevant source sections:** Creation guide section 8; RACI guide section 12.

### Tasks the repository can support

- Reassess authority, delivery and experience after material change.
- Track substantive differences and affected commitments.
- Keep proposals separate from operative wording until adoption.

**Expected output:** A policy review/change pack and adoption decision log.

**Human review:** The designated authority decides substantive revisions; technical/source checks do not constitute adoption.

### Example prompt 1

> Using the RAG, review [current policy, changes and evidence]. Compare obligations, commitments, practice, outcomes and reference versions. Produce proposed revisions, traceable differences, consultation needs and adoption decisions. Preserve current operative text separately and do not invent an approval date.

### Example prompt 2

> Audit [policy revision pack] with the RAG. Find commitments weakened by editorial changes, missing impact review and stale dependencies. Return an approval-readiness assessment with supported, conflicting and unknown items and next human decisions.

## Programme decision and learning record

For each adopted result, record the project/task, source revision and sections used, observed business issue, barrier or opportunity, chosen option and rejected alternatives, expected outcome, evidence available, assumptions, unresolved constraints, accountable person, next check and review trigger. Use actual names and dates when agreed; leave them unassigned when unknown. Record acceptance and residual risk only when the accountable person has made the decision.

Revisit the reference when repository sources change, a new platform or context is introduced, implementation changes invalidate assumptions, or user feedback exposes a barrier. Compare changed sources rather than silently replacing the basis of existing decisions.
