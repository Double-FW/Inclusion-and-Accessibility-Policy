Creating an organisational accessibility and inclusion policy

# Guidance for policy authors and executive sponsors

Version 1.0 | 12 October 2026

A policy should explain how an organisation puts its authorised commitments into practice. It connects what leaders have agreed, what the organisation is required to do, and what its brand promises to the activities, responsibilities and evidence through which these intentions are delivered.

This guide provides a repeatable approach for developing that policy. It is intended for executive sponsors, policy authors, governance teams and the people responsible for customer, employee and operational experiences. It can also guide an AI-assisted drafting process.

## The governing principle

The Executive establishes commitments to organisational change. The policy records and operationalises those commitments within the organisation's delegated authority. Policy drafting does not independently approve new ambitions, expenditure, targets or change programmes. Where a commitment cannot yet be delivered, the gap is made visible for a decision.

Applicable legal and regulatory duties are a separate source of obligation. They do not depend on whether the Executive has restated them. The policy author identifies the relevant duties with the responsible legal or regulatory function and connects them to established governance.

## What the completed work should provide

- A concise draft policy that reflects the organisation's purpose, values, activities and approved commitments.
- A supporting record linking each substantive clause to its authority, delivery process, owner and evidence.
- A separate decision log for missing authority, conflicting commitments, delivery gaps and proposed improvements.
- A defined review and executive reporting cycle that keeps commitments and activity aligned.

## How to use this guide

Work through sections 1 to 4 to establish evidence and draft the policy. Use sections 5 to 8 to connect it to governance, reporting and review. Section 9 provides an AI drafting brief. Sections 10 and 11 map the Double FW resources to this work and identify the source material.

## 1 Learn from the BBC policy

The BBC Digital Product Accessibility Policy connects accessibility to its organisational purpose and Diversity Strategy. It describes the experiences and activities within scope, sets an ambition for comparable experiences, and connects that ambition to research, delivery responsibilities, resources and board reporting. \[1\]

Its practical mechanisms include shared product, design and editorial responsibility; its design system and accessibility guidance; specialist support; a champions network; and provisions for different products and media. These mechanisms are specific to the BBC. The transferable lesson is the relationship between purpose, commitments and practice, rather than copying its operating model. \[1\]

| **Policy feature**                     | **How to apply the approach**                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purpose and organisational ambition        | Use the organisation's own mission, approved commitments and brand values. Explain why accessibility matters to its work. |
| Defined digital activities                 | Identify the actual products, services, environments and relationships covered by the policy.                             |
| Comparable experience and research         | Describe the intended human outcomes and how relevant people contribute evidence.                                         |
| Named delivery mechanisms                  | Connect commitments to processes, guidance, roles and resources that exist or have approval.                              |
| Continuous improvement and board reporting | Specify how evidence reaches decision makers and how decisions return to delivery.                                        |

### Keep four types of document distinct

An executive commitment establishes an authorised intention or outcome. A policy explains how that intention governs organisational activity. Standards and procedures provide the detailed criteria and working instructions. A funded plan identifies the work, milestones and resources needed to close gaps.

A public accessibility statement or service status report has another purpose: it explains the accessibility of a particular service, its known limitations and routes for support. It should agree with the policy without claiming that a policy proves service conformance.

### Avoid importing authority from an example

A BBC provision or a Double FW recommendation does not become an obligation for another organisation merely because it is useful. Treat it as a candidate mechanism. Confirm whether it is already adopted, can be implemented within delegated authority, or needs a new executive decision.

## 2 Assemble the organisational evidence

Begin with a short evidence pack, not a generic policy template. Give each source an identifier, title, owner, date, version or commit reference, approval status and relevant extract. Retain its scope and qualifications. A public promise without a clear delivery owner is an issue to resolve, not a promise to erase.

| **Input**                          | **Questions the author must resolve**                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Executive commitments              | What has been approved, by whom and for which entities or activities? What is aspiration, public promise, funded plan or established practice?                        |
| Regulatory and contractual context | Where does the organisation operate and serve people? Which duties apply to its activities, products, employment, premises and contracts? Who confirms applicability? |
| Brand and strategic objectives     | What values and promises have been adopted? What would customers, employees or visitors experience if these were being honoured?                                      |
| Activities and journeys            | What does the organisation make, buy, deliver and operate? Which customer and employee journeys cross teams or suppliers?                                             |
| Processes and authority            | Which change, procurement, design, HR, facilities, risk and reporting processes already exist? Who owns their decisions?                                              |
| Evidence and participation         | What do testing, research, complaints, adjustment records and lived experience show? Which people, channels or locations are missing?                                 |

### Classify every proposed statement

Use five labels in the working record: applicable external obligation; approved organisational commitment; verified current practice; approved future activity; or proposal awaiting decision. Unknown information remains unknown. Do not describe a planned capability as an existing service.

### Define the boundary explicitly

Record included legal entities, countries, people, channels, products, premises and lifecycle stages. Address contractors, subsidiaries, partnerships and suppliers according to actual control, contracts and duties. Do not assume that a shared brand automatically creates identical authority across every relationship.

An organisation-wide policy can have linked schedules for employment, digital services, premises or particular jurisdictions. A small organisation can use one short policy and one combined register. In either case, document exclusions and their rationale, and check that they do not contradict an applicable duty or approved commitment.

## 3 Translate evidence into policy clauses

### Establish applicable obligations

Ask the responsible legal or regulatory owner to validate an applicability register. Each entry records the authoritative source, jurisdiction, relevant activity, effective date, duty, applicable standard and version where relevant, delivery owner and evidence needed. Record uncertain applicability and the person responsible for resolving it.

Do not assemble a universal list of laws for every organisation. Assess the relevant domains, including employment, service provision, digital products, buildings, procurement, data protection and AI where applicable. Distinguish legislation, regulatory requirements, contracts, adopted standards and voluntary guidance.

If WCAG 2.2 AA is adopted, identify its applicable Level A and AA requirements and the covered content. Keep additional best practice separate. The Double FW embedded model provides such a delivery baseline, but its adoption is an organisational decision, not proof of the legal baseline in every jurisdiction. \[4\]

### Translate values into observable experience

For each approved value, ask what it means for a person trying to complete an actual task. Use the POUR brand lens to consider whether meaning can be perceived, actions performed, choices understood and experiences relied upon. Its emotional framing supports research into confidence, dignity and trust; it does not replace technical conformance evidence. \[5\]

For example, an approved value of independence might be expressed through a customer's ability to complete a purchase privately, using their preferred interaction method. A value of fairness might lead to examining recruitment and progression barriers. These interpretations require validation; they do not authorise new guarantees or targets.

### Build a complete connection

For each commitment, record: source authority → intended outcome → relevant activity → established process → accountable owner → delivery evidence → reporting forum → review trigger. If a link is missing, record the gap and route it to the appropriate owner.

Use this clause pattern: "To give effect to \[approved commitment\], \[accountable function\] uses \[verified or approved process\] within \[scope\]. Progress and unresolved barriers are reported through \[existing forum\], using \[defined evidence\]. Alignment is reviewed at \[approved cadence\] and when \[material trigger\] occurs."

### Resolve conflicts before approval

Where sources disagree, do not silently select the stronger-sounding promise or weaken the existing one. Record both sources, affected people and activities, operational implications and the decision required. Legal questions go to the responsible adviser; commitment, scope and funding decisions go to the Executive or properly delegated authority.

## 4 Structure the draft policy

Use the following structure as a drafting framework. The organisation's language and activities should determine the content. A concise core policy can link to controlled standards, registers and procedures rather than reproduce them.

| **Policy section**                 | **Required content**                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1 Purpose and authority            | Why accessibility and inclusion matter to this organisation; the commitments and external duties the policy gives effect to; approval authority.       |
| 2 Scope                            | People, entities, activities, channels, locations and relationships covered; exclusions and links to domain or local schedules.                        |
| 3 Commitments and outcomes         | The approved commitments, related values and intended experiences. Preserve qualifications and distinguish future ambitions from current arrangements. |
| 4 How commitments are delivered    | The relevant operational processes, shared resources, standards and supplier arrangements; links to controlled instructions.                           |
| 5 Ownership and participation      | Executive sponsorship, operational accountability, consultation with disabled people, specialist support and champions where established.              |
| 6 Change and operational decisions | How relevant commitments enter business cases, procurement, design, implementation, handover and ongoing operations.                                   |
| 7 Evidence and reporting           | Measures, evidence sources, reporting responsibilities, executive forum and escalation route.                                                          |
| 8 Barriers and unresolved gaps     | Accessible feedback and support routes, issue ownership, remediation governance and authorised deviation controls.                                     |
| 9 Review and document control      | Owner, approver, version, effective date, review date, triggers, approval rules and links to supporting records.                                       |

### Write at the right level

Explain responsibilities and decision rules in the policy. Keep tool versions, test scripts, detailed checklists and changing project milestones in linked controlled documents. Identify who approves revisions to these supporting documents so a standards update cannot silently expand or reduce the policy's commitments.

Use "we do" only for verified practice, and "we will" only for an approved commitment or plan. Do not claim universal accessibility, complete legal compliance or a maturity level without evidence supporting the exact scope of the claim.

## 5 Connect policy to change programmes

Map the policy into existing change management and delivery processes. The policy records how authorised commitments remain visible when plans change. It should not introduce an unapproved transformation programme under the cover of a drafting exercise. The Double FW embedded and organisational governance resources provide useful operating models. \[4, 10\]

| **Existing decision point** | **Connection to the policy**                                                                                                                    |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strategy and business case  | Identify affected commitments and people, intended outcomes, known barriers and resources already authorised. Escalate unfunded delivery gaps.  |
| Procurement and partnership | Carry approved requirements into briefs, evaluation, contracts and acceptance evidence within the organisation's authority.                     |
| Discovery and design        | Include relevant perspectives and functional needs; connect research questions and acceptance conditions to the intended outcomes.              |
| Delivery and change control | Retain commitment identifiers in requirements and decision records. Assess inclusion impact when scope, timing, supplier or technology changes. |
| Release or handover         | Use the agreed evidence and decision authority. Record unresolved barriers, support arrangements and operational ownership.                     |
| Operation and improvement   | Connect complaints, research and incidents to the backlog or improvement plan, then verify whether changes improved the experience.             |

### Prevent silent loss of commitments

Where the Executive has committed to protecting inclusion from descoping, state how the existing change authority enforces that commitment. A delivery team should not quietly remove an agreed outcome to meet a deadline. Record the impact, alternatives, decision owner and escalation. Do not imply that a new policy author can create a release veto or funding authority.

Routine implementation can proceed within existing delegation. A change to the substance of an executive commitment returns to the authority that owns it. An applicable duty is not suspended by an internal risk acceptance or delayed policy update.

### Keep deviations visible

For any permitted internal deviation, record the source requirement, affected users, reason, evidence, alternatives, interim access arrangements, accountable decision maker, actions and review or expiry date. Check the legal position separately. A support route may help someone now without demonstrating an equivalent experience or closing the underlying barrier.

Where no delivery mechanism exists, retain the commitment and document the gap. Describe only the approved response in the policy; put unapproved mechanisms, costs and milestones in the decision log.

## 6 Assign ownership and involve people

Use the organisation's existing role names and delegation arrangements. A small organisation may combine roles, but each decision or deliverable still needs one clear accountable owner. RACI means Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed; it describes allocation rather than creating authority.

| **Activity**                                    | **Accountable**                      | **Responsible**                  | **Consulted**                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Approve commitments and material policy changes | Executive or board authority         | Policy owner                     | Legal and regulatory owners; function leads; affected people |
| Draft and maintain policy                       | Designated policy owner              | Author or governance coordinator | Operational owners; disabled participants; specialists       |
| Validate obligation applicability               | Designated legal or regulatory owner | Relevant adviser                 | Policy owner; affected functions                             |
| Deliver an agreed outcome                       | Relevant function or service owner   | Delivery and operational teams   | Affected users; specialists; champions                       |
| Compile executive reporting                     | Executive sponsor                    | Evidence owners and coordinator  | Research, data and risk owners                               |
| Review alignment and propose revisions          | Policy owner                         | Coordinator and function owners  | Executive sponsor; affected people; advisers                 |

These are proposed allocations for confirmation. Name the Informed audience for each activity in the organisation's full RACI, including teams and suppliers affected by approved changes. Do not assign executive accountability to an AI system.

### Participation is part of the evidence

Include disabled and neurodivergent people in developing and reviewing the policy, using accessible participation methods. Ask whether the stated commitments match their experience, whether support routes work, and whether reporting would expose the barriers that matter to them. Record whose perspectives are missing.

Agree access support, appropriate recognition or compensation, privacy arrangements and a feedback route explaining what happened to participants' contributions. Do not require unnecessary disclosure or treat one employee network as representing every experience.

### Specialists and champions support embedded ownership

Where established, an accessibility manager or team can maintain guidance, develop capability, challenge evidence and coordinate cross-functional learning. Champions can bring accessibility into everyday decisions and escalate threats to approved commitments. Executive sponsorship and management support give that work practical influence. \[4, 6\]

Neither specialists nor champions remove delivery accountability from function owners. A proposed network needs approval, time, support and a clear remit before the policy describes it as operational.

## 7 Report delivery and human outcomes

Executive reporting should answer three questions: Are we delivering our commitments? What are people experiencing? What decisions are needed? Connect every reported item to a commitment or applicable obligation, an accountable owner and the activity in scope.

### A minimum executive report

- Coverage: commitments mapped to owners and processes, with gaps and unknown coverage visible.
- Delivery: approved milestones, completed work, overdue actions and dependencies.
- Experience: evidence of completion, independence, effort, confidence, satisfaction and unresolved exclusion.
- Assurance: applicable conformance evidence, testing and research coverage, limitations and recurring barriers.
- Decisions: funding, priorities, policy conflicts or changes requiring authority, with options and consequences.

### Measures must explain what they mean

For each measure, define its scope, baseline, denominator, source, collection method, owner, reporting frequency and any approved target. Keep targets awaiting agreement separate. Describe missing data as unknown rather than satisfactory.

Use technical checks alongside manual evaluation and user evidence. Completion alone can conceal excessive effort, dependence on colleagues or loss of privacy. The comparative task-analysis resource helps examine these differences in internal systems. The design perspectives resource connects functional outcomes with emotional experience. \[11, 12\]

For example, report successful completion of a defined task by relevant interaction method, together with time or effort, assistance needed, study context and uncertainty. For workplace adjustments, examine whether the arrangement works, not simply how many requests have been closed. Training attendance and champions counts measure activity, not proof of inclusion.

### Protect the people behind the data

Collect only the information needed for the agreed purpose. Use voluntary and appropriate segmentation, protect small groups from identification, and report limitations. Do not infer disability from assistive technology use or behaviour. Evaluate barriers in systems rather than use task comparisons to rank disabled employees.

### Connect existing governance views

The AI Accessibility Maturity Model provides six dimensions for examining organisational capability; product test results alone cannot establish maturity. Report dimension-level evidence and gaps without averaging away serious weaknesses. The ESG mapping can connect verified social outcomes and governance evidence to existing reporting; it is not a formal ESG reporting standard or a certification. \[9, 13\]

## 8 Review alignment and manage revisions

Review tests whether the policy still accurately connects commitments, obligations, values and activity. It also tests whether the chosen mechanisms work. Rewording a policy is not a substitute for fixing a delivery gap.

### Set a proportionate cycle

Use an existing governance rhythm where possible. An annual full policy review, quarterly executive review of delivery and outcomes, and earlier review after material change provide a practical starting proposal. Confirm the cadence through the organisation's authority before presenting it as established practice.

Trigger an earlier review when executive commitments or brand values change; a relevant duty or contract changes; an acquisition, new market, service or premises changes scope; AI or a supplier changes a material workflow; or user evidence exposes persistent exclusion. Significant incidents and repeated deviations should also prompt review.

### Use a repeatable review sequence

- Refresh the source and applicability registers, including versions and approval status.
- Compare every substantive clause with its current authority, operational process and owner.
- Examine outcome evidence with affected people and identify gaps, conflicts and ineffective mechanisms.
- Classify each finding as an editorial correction, delivery issue, proposed process change or proposed change to commitment.
- Route decisions to the existing authority; record approval, rationale and effective date.
- Update linked procedures, reporting, training and supplier instructions, then confirm adoption and effectiveness.

### Keep the authority boundary intact

The policy owner may make editorial corrections within delegated powers. Operational owners address implementation within their remit. Changes to ambition, substantive scope, targets, resource commitments or decision rights need the relevant approving authority. Record a proposal as a proposal until approved.

Where a commitment remains valid but delivery is weak, keep the commitment visible and escalate the gap. Do not lower the policy ambition solely to improve reported performance. Where an approved commitment changes, preserve the previous version and explain the consequences for existing plans and public statements.

### Check readiness for approval

Confirm that every material clause has a source; obligations have been validated; current and future activity are distinguished; owners accept their responsibilities; affected people have been consulted; and gaps have explicit decisions or clearly recorded unresolved status. Check accessible publication, meaningful headings and links, version control and a working contact route.

## 9 Use a controlled drafting brief

The following brief can be given to a policy author or an AI assistant with the evidence pack. Its output is a draft for review, not authorisation to change organisational practice.

### Reusable drafting instruction

Create a draft accessibility and inclusion policy for \[organisation\]. Use the supplied approved commitments, validated applicability register, brand values, activity and journey inventory, current processes, delegation records and evidence of people's experiences. Use the structure in section 4.

Reflect the organisation's own objectives, vocabulary and activities. Trace every substantive clause to its source identifier. Preserve the scope, qualifications and approval status of each commitment. Separate applicable external obligations from voluntary commitments, verified current practice, approved future activity and unapproved proposals.

Connect each commitment to an intended outcome, relevant activity, established or approved process, accountable owner, evidence, reporting route and review trigger. Use the Double FW resources as implementation references. Do not treat their recommendations, BBC arrangements or AI-generated suggestions as adopted organisational policy.

Do not invent a legal conclusion, owner, budget, deadline, target, certification, network, process or capability. Where evidence is missing, use a clearly marked placeholder and enter the question in a separate decision log. Do not silently remove existing commitments because the delivery mechanism is absent.

Identify conflicts and material gaps. Explain the decision needed and the appropriate authority from the supplied delegation records. Keep proposed changes outside operative policy wording until approval. Do not replace disabled people's evidence with synthetic personas or assumptions.

Return four outputs: the draft policy; a clause-to-source and delivery register; a decision and gap log; and an approval and review checklist. Clearly identify which material is ready for review and which depends on unresolved decisions.

### Supporting record formats

Clause record: clause ID; source ID and exact location; authority type and status; scope; intended outcome; activity; process reference; accountable owner; evidence and measure; reporting forum; review trigger.

Decision record: issue ID; affected clauses and people; missing or conflicting evidence; current obligation or commitment; options and implications; authorised decision maker; decision status; agreed actions; review date.

### Review AI output before adoption

A human policy owner checks provenance and organisational accuracy. Relevant advisers validate obligation claims, and operational owners confirm practice and feasibility. The designated authority approves the policy. AI can assist with drafting, comparison and monitoring; it cannot independently create executive commitments or certify compliance.

## 10 Apply the approach in practice

### An illustrative commitment carried into delivery

Assume an organisation has approved the commitment "Customers should be able to manage their account independently", has adopted "respect for people's time" as a brand value, and already operates a service change process and quarterly executive service review. These are illustrative inputs, not commitments proposed for every organisation.

The author maps the commitment to account registration, authentication, changes and recovery. Research shows that one recovery route needs assistance. The service owner confirms that recovery improvements can enter the existing change process, but a new completion target has not been approved.

A supported draft clause would say: "Our commitment to independent account management is delivered through the service change process. The service owner uses accessibility evaluation, customer research and operational feedback to assess account journeys and track unresolved barriers. Progress is considered in the quarterly executive service review."

The accompanying record identifies the approved commitment, process and forum; the recovery barrier; available evidence; and the owner. Any proposed target or additional funding goes in the decision log. The policy does not claim that independent recovery is already available.

## 11 Select operational references

| **Resource**                                | **Use and boundary**                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Accessible workspaces \[7\]                 | Review arrival, routes, work areas, facilities and emergency/support arrangements. Its England-oriented guidance needs local applicability checks.             |
| Accommodation checklist \[8\]               | Apply to relevant rooms and stays, including visiting professionals. This concerns accommodation environments, not the complete employment adjustment process. |
| AI Accessibility Maturity Model \[9\]       | Assess leadership; culture; delivery; measurement; AI; and ethics. Establish evidence and gaps across all six dimensions.                                      |
| Organisation governance and strategy \[10\] | Connect policy to strategy, change management, procurement, operational governance and executive scorecards.                                                   |
| Product design perspectives \[11\]          | Use functional and emotional outcome questions and mixed methods. Its sighted/nonvisual perspectives do not cover every disability experience.                 |
| Comparative task analysis \[12\]            | Assess independence, effort, efficiency and cognitive burden in internal systems, with real-user evidence and context.                                         |
| Accessibility maturity and ESG \[13\]       | Connect credible inclusion outcomes and governance evidence to existing ESG reporting without unsupported sustainability claims.                               |

### Maintain an adopted reference register

Record each selected document, exact version or commit, relevant sections, owner and approval date. Review upstream changes before adoption. A live repository link should not automatically alter an organisation's obligations, release criteria or executive commitments.

The repositories may offer different frameworks for different purposes. Keep their terminology and evidence boundaries clear. For example, an ESG mapping, a technical test result and a maturity assessment answer different questions and should not be combined into an unexplained single score.

### Keep the policy specific to the organisation

Only include mechanisms that relate to its activities and authority. An organisation without accommodation services may have no need for an accommodation schedule; an employer still needs to assess its employment and workplace responsibilities. A business using AI in recruitment needs to examine that workflow even if its digital product team does not use AI.

The final test is whether a reader can follow a real commitment from its authority to the work that gives effect to it, the evidence of people's experience, and the executive decision that follows when delivery falls short.
